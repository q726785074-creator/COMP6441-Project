Know the method

Phishing borrows trust.

Social engineering

Phishing is an attempt to make someone reveal information, send money, open harmful content, or grant access by pretending to be a trusted person or service.

Eight focused lessons

Open a topic to examine the attacker, the signal, and the control.

Each lesson connects what a student can observe with the technical and organisational controls that reduce risk.

Security engineering lens

Analyse the system, not only the message.

Phishing succeeds when an attacker crosses a trust boundary by making an untrusted request appear to come from a trusted university service.

ASSETS

What needs protection?

Credentials, authenticated sessions, personal data, university systems, money, and the student's ability to study.

THREAT ACTOR

What does the attacker want?

Account access, financial gain, malware execution, or a trusted account that can be used to target others.

TRUST BOUNDARY

Where does trust fail?

Email display names, links, and copied branding can cross from an external sender into a trusted-looking interface.

FAILURE MODE

Why can one clue fail?

Legitimate messages can be urgent, while malicious messages can have perfect spelling and HTTPS. Controls must use layered evidence.

Impersonate→Create pressure→Redirect or attach→Capture access→Exploit account

Threat, control, and residual risk

A control reduces risk; it does not erase the attack path.

These cases connect assets and attacker actions with layered controls, remaining exposure, and operational trade-offs.

Defensive strategy

Respond without taking the bait.

Four safe moves
01

Pause

Do not click, reply, open the attachment, or call a number supplied in the suspicious message.

02

Inspect

Check the full sender address, real link destination, request, context, and attachment type. Use several signals.

03

Verify independently

Open the official university portal yourself or contact support using a number or address you already trust.

04

Report

Use the university's official phishing-reporting process, then delete the message after preserving any required evidence.

Choose before reading the explanation.

Seven response decisions

Select one response.
Remember

A polished message, correct grammar, familiar logo, or HTTPS connection does not prove legitimacy. When uncertain, verify through a separate trusted channel.

Layered controls

Defence is a system responsibility.

No single user, filter, or authentication method can stop every phishing attack. Strong systems combine controls before, during, and after an incident.

PREVENT

Reduce the opportunity

Secure email configuration, domain protection, phishing-resistant MFA, safe defaults, and clear communication practices.

DETECT

Find suspicious activity

Email filtering, user reporting, unusual-login alerts, domain monitoring, and authentication logs.

RESPOND

Limit the harm

Password reset through the official service, session revocation, incident reporting, device checks, and support without victim blaming.

RECOVER

Restore safely

Recover clean accounts and devices, communicate with affected users, review root causes, and improve controls.

Ethics & safety

Practice must not create harm.

  • 01
    No real attacks

    Every identity, message, URL, and attachment in this activity is fictional and inert.

  • 02
    No sensitive data

    Never enter a real password into a training simulation. This site asks for no credentials or personal details.

  • 03
    No external transmission

    There are no accounts, analytics, network requests, or server submissions.

  • 04
    No victim blaming

    Phishing exploits normal trust and attention. Reporting mistakes quickly should be encouraged.

  • 05
    No guarantee

    Indicators support risk decisions but cannot prove that every message is safe or malicious.

Teaching effectiveness

A working activity is the start of evaluation, not the result.

Formal evidence should follow a consented and repeatable process. Demo values remain presentation examples and must stay outside the participant dataset.
